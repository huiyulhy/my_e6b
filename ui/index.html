      Summary

      Go / no-go

      Each runway is checked end by end at the field's wind: the end with the better headwind is the one the distances are read for, and its crosswind is checked against the 15 kt maximum demonstrated. A gust counts against you — for the crosswind and for a tailwind — and never for you. Get weather, over the navlog, fills each field's wind, temperature and altimeter setting from its current report; with no wind entered the distances are the zero-wind book figures, which every runway says beside itself. Landing distance is the POH max-weight figure. An unknown runway is never treated as a pass. A query past the edge of a chart is refused, or answered from the nearest published air and listed as extrapolated — with the navlog row it first appears on, since time and fuel are cumulative from there. Cruise is the chart's ISA ±20 columns at the row's own pressure altitude; outside that band there is no published column for the operating point, so the reading is an extrapolation however close the substituted air is.

      Navlog consistency

      Checks the log against itself — altitudes that do not join up, a leg that does not do what it says. Separate from go/no-go: that says whether the airplane can do it, this says whether the plan agrees with itself.

      E6B

      Density altitude

      Pressure altitude from the setting, density altitude from that and the temperature — the same functions the navigation log reads performance at.

      Magnetic variation

      Decimal degrees, south and west negative. East is positive: subtract it from a true course to get magnetic.

      Weight & balance

      Weights in pounds, arms in inches aft of the datum. Gross weight is the sum; the CG is the total moment divided by it. No envelope is checked — compare both against the POH for your aircraft.
